Common name: Kans Grass, Wild Sugarcane
Scientific name: Saccharum spontaneum L.
Family: Poaceae

Habitat: Native throughout much of tropical and subtropical Asia, northern Australia, and eastern and northern Africa. A perennial grass growing up to three metres tall with spreading rhizomatous roots.

Economic Importance: A wild relative of cultivated sugarcane; it has hybridized with Saccharum officinarum to help produce cultivated sugarcane varieties such as Saccharum barberi and Saccharum sinense.

Biodiversity Value:
- Extensive rhizomes bind loose river-bank and floodplain soil, reducing erosion during monsoon floods.
- Provides cover and nesting habitat for birds and small wildlife in riverine grasslands.
