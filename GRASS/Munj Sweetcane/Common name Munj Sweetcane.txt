Common name: Munj Sweetcane, Baruwa Sugarcane, Baruwa Grass
Scientific name: Tripidium bengalense (Retz.) H.Scholz (synonym: Saccharum bengalense)
Family: Poaceae

Habitat: Native to Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan, northern India, Nepal, Bangladesh and Myanmar, with a primary native range in northeastern India (Assam) within the Terai-Duar grasslands at the foothills of the Himalayas. A small sugarcane-like grass, 2-3 feet tall, pinkish-green in colour. Food source for the Indian rhinoceros and the pygmy hog.

Economic Importance: Widely used as a raw material for thatching roofs, basketmaking and hand fans, and its fibre is used for making ropes, mats and shields. An ecologically successful native colonizer of abandoned mines and rocky habitats with skeletal soils; its extensive root network binds soil and pebbles, making it useful for stabilizing erosion-prone slopes.
