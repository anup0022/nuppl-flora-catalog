Common name: Japanese Stiltgrass
Scientific name: Microstegium vimineum (Trin.) A. Camus
Family: Poaceae

Habitat: Native to Asia from Turkey to Japan, including China, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines and the Himalayas. An annual grass 40-100 cm tall, well adapted to low light levels, rooting at each stem node. Introduced widely in North America where it invades roadsides, floodplains and disturbed or shaded forest understory.

Economic Importance: Considered a significant invasive weed outside its native range; suppresses native plant growth and flowering, alters insect communities and nutrient cycling, and slows forest succession. Controlled mainly by mowing before it sets seed. Provides some ecological benefit as a host plant for certain native satyr butterflies and cover for amphibians such as wood frogs and toads in invaded areas.
