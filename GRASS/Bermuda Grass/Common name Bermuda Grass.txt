Common name: Bermuda Grass, Durva Grass, Doob, Couch Grass
Scientific name: Cynodon dactylon (L.) Pers.
Family: Poaceae

Habitat: Native to Europe, Africa, Australia and much of Asia, and introduced to the Americas; a warm-season grass found worldwide in warm and tropical climates. Has a deep root system that can reach over 2 metres in penetrable soil under drought conditions, and creeps along the ground via stolons and rhizomes, rooting wherever a node touches soil. Becomes dormant and turns light brown in winter or the dry season; grows best in full sun.

Uses: A popular, highly desirable turfgrass in warm-temperate climates, widely used for lawns, sports fields and golf courses because of its fast growth and quick recovery from damage. Cultivated in saline soils, such as in California's Central Valley, and used to graze cattle. Can also become a highly aggressive invasive weed, sometimes called "devil grass".

Biodiversity Value:
- Commonly known as durva in India, it is considered sacred in Hinduism and used in the Ayurveda system of medicine.
- Offered during worship of Lord Ganesha and features in Hindu wedding and festival rituals across India, Nepal and elsewhere in South Asia.

Medicinal Importance: The rhizomes are reported to act as a diuretic and the grass juice as an astringent in traditional use.
