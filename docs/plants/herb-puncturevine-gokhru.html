Puncturevine Gokhru

Also known as: Puncturevine, Gokhru, Goathead, Caltrop

KingdomPlantae
FamilyZygophyllaceae
GenusTribulus
SpeciesTribulus terrestris
Scientific nameTribulus terrestris L.
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • A taprooted annual herb widespread from latitudes 35 degrees S to 47 degrees N, native to warm temperate and tropical regions of southern Europe, southern Asia and Africa, and now naturalized across the Americas and Australia. Stems radiate from the crown into flat prostrate patches 10 cm to over 1 m wide; thrives in dry, loose, sandy soils and disturbed roadside ground. The hard, spiny burs readily puncture bicycle tyres and bare feet, giving the plant its common names.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Considered a noxious agricultural weed in many regions because its spiny burs damage livestock (mouths and wool) and are difficult to eradicate; young leaves and shoots are eaten as a vegetable in parts of East Asia.

Medicinal Importance

  • The fruit and extract have been used since the 1980s as a dietary supplement marketed for boosting testosterone and athletic performance, though controlled studies have not consistently supported these claims.
