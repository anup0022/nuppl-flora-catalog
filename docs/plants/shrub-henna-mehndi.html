Henna Mehndi

Also known as: Henna, Mehndi, Egyptian Privet

KingdomPlantae
FamilyLythraceae
GenusLawsonia
SpeciesLawsonia inermis
Scientific nameLawsonia inermis L.
CategoryShrubs
IUCN Red ListLeast Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List LC

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to northern and eastern Africa, Asia and northern Australia in semi-arid and tropical zones; a tall, glabrous, multi-branched shrub or small tree 1.8-7.6 m high with spine-tipped branchlets and elliptical, lanceolate leaves. Produces the most dye when grown at 35-45 degrees C and cannot tolerate temperatures below about 5 degrees C.

Biodiversity Value

  • Fragrant flowers attract bees and moths; thorny branches offer safe nesting cover for small birds.
  • Deep roots and dense branching help stabilise soil in semi-arid hedgerows.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • The dried leaves are the source of the traditional henna dye used across South Asia, the Middle East and North Africa to colour skin, hair, nails, silk, wool and leather; commercially cultivated on a large scale for this purpose.

Medicinal Importance

  • Contains the compound lawsone, and preliminary studies suggest antioxidant, antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties, though clinical safety and efficacy in humans remain under study; henna is known to pose a risk to individuals with G6PD deficiency.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference

  • Classical Ayurvedic name is Madayantika; documented in traditional Nighantu texts as a cooling dravya used for skin and hair care.
