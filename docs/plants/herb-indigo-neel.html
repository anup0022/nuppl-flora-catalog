Herbs Specimen

Indigo Neel

Also known as: Indigo, Neel, True Indigo

KingdomPlantae
FamilyFabaceae
GenusIndigofera
SpeciesIndigofera tinctoria
Scientific nameIndigofera tinctoria L.
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • A shrub 1-2 m high, native to tropical West Africa, Tanzania to South Africa and the Indian subcontinent to mainland Southeast Asia; may grow as an annual, biennial or perennial depending on climate, with light green pinnate leaves and pink or violet flowers.

Biodiversity Value

  • As a legume, root nodules fix nitrogen and improve soil fertility for neighbouring plants.
  • Flowers attract bees and butterflies; historically grown across large plantation areas, shaping regional farmland.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • One of the original historic sources of indigo dye, obtained by fermenting the leaves to convert the glycoside indican into the blue pigment indigotin; still marketed today as a natural dye (known as tarum in Indonesia and nila in Malaysia) alongside its much larger-scale synthetic replacement. As a legume it is also grown as a soil-improving groundcover and rotated into fields to enrich the soil.

Medicinal Importance

  • Leaves and root used in Ayurveda for skin diseases and liver disorders; powdered leaf traditionally applied for hair and scalp health.
