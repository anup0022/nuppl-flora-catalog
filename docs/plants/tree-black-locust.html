Trees Specimen

Black Locust

Also known as: Black Locust, False Acacia

KingdomPlantae
FamilyFabaceae
GenusRobinia
SpeciesRobinia pseudoacacia
Scientific nameRobinia pseudoacacia L.
CategoryTrees

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to two separate populations in the eastern United States, centred on the Appalachian Mountains and the Ozark-Ouachita region, now naturalized across temperate North America, Europe, Asia (including India and Pakistan) and parts of Africa. A medium-sized deciduous tree 12-30 m tall with deeply furrowed reddish-black bark, paired short spines at leaf bases, and fragrant cream-white pea-like flowers borne in drooping racemes 10-20 cm long. Its root nodules fix nitrogen, allowing it to colonise poor, disturbed and sandy soils as a pioneer species.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Highly valued as one of the most decay-resistant hardwoods in North America, used for fence posts, flooring, furniture and shipbuilding trunnels; also an important honey plant, yielding the prized "acacia honey" in Europe, and widely planted for erosion control and firewood due to its fast growth and high heat content.

Medicinal Importance

  • In traditional Indian medicine, various parts of the tree have been used as a laxative, antispasmodic and diuretic; however, the bark, leaves and wood contain the toxalbumin robin and are toxic to humans and livestock if ingested raw.
