Herbs Specimen

Coatbuttons

Also known as: Coatbuttons, Tridax Daisy

KingdomPlantae
FamilyAsteraceae
GenusTridax
SpeciesTridax procumbens
Scientific nameTridax procumbens L.
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to the tropical Americas including Mexico, now introduced to tropical, subtropical and mild temperate regions worldwide. Found in fields, meadows, croplands, disturbed areas, lawns and roadsides; listed as a noxious weed in several US states due to its prolific, wind-dispersed seed production.

Biodiversity Value

  • Small daisy-like flowers are a reliable nectar source for bees and butterflies almost year-round.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Considered a noxious cropland weed due to prolific seeding, but its ground cover limits erosion on wasteland.

Medicinal Importance

  • Traditionally used in India for wound healing, as an anticoagulant, antifungal and insect repellent, and by local healers as a treatment for boils, blisters and cuts. Young leaves are crushed to yield a green juice applied directly to wounds to stop bleeding, including after leech bites.
