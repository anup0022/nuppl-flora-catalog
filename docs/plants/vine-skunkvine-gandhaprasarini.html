Skunkvine Gandhaprasarini

Also known as: Skunkvine, Gandhaprasarini, Stinkvine, Chinese Fever Vine

KingdomPlantae
FamilyRubiaceae
GenusPaederia
SpeciesPaederia foetida
Scientific namePaederia foetida L.
CategoryVines / Climbers

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to temperate and tropical Asia, including Bangladesh, Bhutan, Cambodia, China and India; naturalized in the Mascarenes, Melanesia, Polynesia and Hawaii. Known for the strong sulphurous odour released when its leaves or stems are crushed or bruised, caused by sulphur compounds including dimethyl disulphide in the leaf oil.

Uses

  • Sometimes planted as an ornamental. Used as a culinary spice in traditional cooking of northeastern and eastern India; in Hainanese cuisine the leaves are ground into flour and mixed with rice for a sweet noodle soup.

Biodiversity Value

  • Small, sulphur-scented flowers attract specific pollinating insects while the odour deters most grazing animals.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Used as both a traditional medicine and a culinary herb in parts of Asia, though it can become a smothering, invasive vine elsewhere.

Medicinal Importance

  • Has a recognized place in folk medicine across its native range, though specific claimed effects vary by region.
