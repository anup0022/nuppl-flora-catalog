Curry Leaf Tree (Meetha Neem)

Also known as: Curry Leaf Tree / Meetha Neem / Kadi Patta / Kariveppilai

KingdomPlantae
GenusMurraya
SpeciesMurraya koenigii
Scientific nameMurraya koenigii
TypeAromatic shrub to small tree, family Rutaceae
CategoryTrees

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to India and Sri Lanka, growing wild in the foothills of the Himalayas, the Western Ghats, and across tropical-subtropical India. Thrives in hot, humid tropical to subtropical climate at 25-40°C; sensitive to frost, with leaves dropping in severe winter though the plant survives and flushes back in spring. Needs full sun to partial shade for best growth and aroma (minimum 4-6 hours daily), though leaves in shade have a weaker flavour. Prefers well-drained, fertile loamy soil (pH 6.0-7.0), tolerating slightly alkaline soil but not waterlogging or heavy clay. Planted in home gardens, farm boundaries, and pots, and also grows wild along roadsides and in open forests, self-seeding from bird-dropped seeds.

Biodiversity Value

  • Planted in home gardens, farm boundaries, and pots, and also grows wild along roadsides and in open forests, self-seeding from bird-dropped seeds.
  • Host plant for Common Mormon and Lime Butterfly caterpillars; flowers attract bees.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Culinary: Main use; fresh leaves are essential in Indian cooking as a tadka for dal, curries, chutneys, upma, and poha, with their distinct aroma from essential oils. Leaves are sold fresh in all Indian markets, and dried powder is also marketed.
  • Commercial crop: Cultivated commercially in South India, especially Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Karnataka, with Coimbatore a major market; leaves harvested every 2-3 months once established.
  • Cosmetic: Leaf extract used in hair oils and shampoos for preventing greying and hair fall; "kadi patta hair oil" is popular.

Medicinal Importance

  • Used in Ayurveda and folk medicine, with leaves said to help with diabetes, cholesterol, digestion, hair health, and anaemia; contains carbazole alkaloids, antioxidants, and iron. Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use.

Field Notes

  • Stems used as datun for teeth cleaning in villages; the hard wood is used for tool handles.
