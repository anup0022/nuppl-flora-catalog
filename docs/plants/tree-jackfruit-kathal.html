Jackfruit Kathal

Also known as: Jackfruit, Kathal

KingdomPlantae
FamilyMoraceae
GenusArtocarpus
SpeciesArtocarpus heterophyllus
Scientific nameArtocarpus heterophyllus Lam.
CategoryTrees

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • An evergreen tree reaching 9-21 m tall, likely originating in the Western Ghats of India and now widely cultivated across South and Southeast Asia and tropical regions worldwide. Flowers and fruit are borne directly on the trunk and branches (cauliflory); the jackfruit is the largest tree-borne fruit, weighing up to 55 kg.

Biodiversity Value

  • Large trunk-borne fruit is eaten by civets, monkeys, birds and bats, which help disperse the seeds.
  • Dense canopy offers shade and nesting sites for birds in home gardens and orchards.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • A major food crop across South and Southeast Asia; unripe fruit is cooked as a savoury vegetable and meat substitute, ripe fruit is eaten fresh or in desserts, and the seeds are boiled or roasted and eaten. India is the world's leading producer. The termite-resistant golden timber is prized for furniture and musical instruments including the veena and mridangam.

Medicinal Importance

  • Leaves, latex and roots used in traditional medicine for wounds, diarrhoea and skin conditions.
