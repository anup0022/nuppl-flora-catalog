Darbha Grass

Also known as: Darbha Grass, Halfa Grass, Big Cordgrass, Salt Reed-grass

KingdomPlantae
FamilyPoaceae
GenusDesmostachya
SpeciesDesmostachya bipinnata
Scientific nameDesmostachya bipinnata (L.) Stapf
CategoryGrasses
IUCN Red ListLeast Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List LC

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to northeast and west tropical and northern Africa, the Middle East, and temperate to tropical Asia including India, Iran, Pakistan and Nepal. A tall, tufted, leafy perennial grass branching from a stout creeping rootstock; also occurs as a weed in wheat crops.

Biodiversity Value

  • Known as darbha or kusha in Sanskrit, it has long been considered a sacred plant in Hindu, Jain and Buddhist traditions; the Rigveda mentions it for use in sacred ceremonies and as a seat for priests and deities, and it is specifically recommended by Krishna in the Bhagavad Gita as part of the ideal meditation seat.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Known as darbha or kusha in Sanskrit, it has long been considered a sacred plant in Hindu, Jain and Buddhist traditions; the Rigveda mentions it for use in sacred ceremonies and as a seat for priests and deities, and it is specifically recommended by Krishna in the Bhagavad Gita as part of the ideal meditation seat. Rope made from this grass was found in the Khufu ship, used both for mooring and to help hold the ship's construction together. Used as fodder for livestock in arid regions.

Medicinal Importance

  • Used in folk medicine to treat dysentery and menorrhagia, and as a diuretic.
