Trees Specimen

Shahtoot Mulberry

Also known as: Shahtoot Mulberry / Mulberry / Toot

KingdomPlantae
FamilyMoraceae
GenusMorus
SpeciesMorus alba
Scientific nameMorus alba
TypeDeciduous shrub or small tree
CategoryTrees

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to northern China but widely naturalized across India. Very common in UP, including Kanpur region
  • Thrives in warm temperate to subtropical climate. Tolerates heat of UP summers and light frost in winter
  • Grows best in full sun but tolerates partial shade. Deciduous - sheds leaves in winter
  • Prefers deep, fertile, well-drained loamy soil. Very adaptable - tolerates poor soil, drought, and pollution once established
  • Planted in home gardens, farms, roadsides, and field boundaries. Also found wild/escaped near villages
  • Fast growing. Can be coppiced/pruned heavily and regrows quickly
  • Small to medium deciduous tree with a spreading crown and milky sap
  • Leaves are alternate, glossy green, and often variable in shape, with lobed and unlobed forms on the same tree
  • Fruits are elongated aggregate berries that ripen from white or pink to deep purple-black, depending on the variety
  • Male and female flowers are small and usually borne in separate catkins; some cultivated trees are dioecious

Uses

  • Bark used for paper in ancient times. Fruit yields natural dye

Biodiversity Value

  • Primary host plant for silkworms Bombyx mori.
  • Used for afforestation, soil conservation, and as windbreaks.
  • Fruits provide food for birds, insects, and small mammals.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Sericulture: Primary host plant for silkworms Bombyx mori. Leaves are the sole food for silkworms. Backbone of India’s silk industry. UP has sericulture farms
  • Fruit: Produces sweet, juicy berries - white, pink, or black when ripe. Eaten fresh, dried, or made into jam, juice, and wine. Rich in vitamin C, iron, and antioxidants

Medicinal Importance

  • In Ayurveda and Traditional Chinese Medicine, leaves, bark, fruit, and root used for diabetes, fever, cough, blood pressure, and as a coolant. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
  • Fodder: Leaves are highly nutritious fodder for goats, sheep, and cattle. Rich in protein
  • Timber: Wood is hard and durable. Used for furniture, sports goods, tool handles, and fuelwood
  • Environmental: Used for afforestation, soil conservation, and as windbreaks. Tolerates pollution, so planted in cities
  • Leaves, bark, fruit and root used in Ayurveda and Traditional Chinese Medicine for diabetes, fever and cough.

Field Notes

  • Conservation Status: Least Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List. Endemic to China.
