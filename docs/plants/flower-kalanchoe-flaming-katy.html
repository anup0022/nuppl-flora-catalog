Flowering / Ornamental Specimen

Kalanchoe Flaming Katy

Also known as: Kalanchoe / Flaming Katy

KingdomPlantae
FamilyCrassulaceae
GenusKalanchoe
SpeciesKalanchoe blossfeldiana
Scientific nameKalanchoe blossfeldiana
TypeSucculent perennial herb
CategoryFlowering / Ornamental

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to the rocky, well-drained slopes of Madagascar, and now grown as a pot and bedding plant across the tropics and subtropics, including throughout India.
  • Prefers 15-28 degrees C; growth stalls below about 10 degrees C and the fleshy leaves are damaged by frost, so it is kept indoors or on sheltered balconies during North Indian winters.
  • Needs bright light with three to five hours of gentle direct sun; in deep shade the stems stretch, the leaves pale and flowering is sparse.
  • Requires a gritty, free-draining potting mix. The thick roots rot quickly in heavy or waterlogged soil, so it should be watered only once the top few centimetres have dried out.
  • A short-day plant: flower buds are initiated only after about six weeks of long nights, which is why it blooms naturally from late winter into spring.
  • As a succulent it uses CAM photosynthesis, opening its stomata at night to conserve moisture, which makes it unusually tolerant of dry indoor air.

Uses

  • Compact evergreen succulent, usually 20-40 cm tall, with thick glossy dark-green leaves in opposite pairs and distinctly scalloped margins.
  • Small four-petalled flowers are packed into dense flat-topped clusters held well above the foliage, in scarlet, red, pink, orange, yellow or white.
  • A single flush of flower heads can remain colourful for several weeks, giving it one of the longest displays of any flowering pot plant.
  • Propagated easily from stem or leaf cuttings, left to callus for a day before being set into a sandy mix.
  • After flowering, spent heads are cut back and the plant is given a spell of long, uninterrupted nights to trigger the next flush.

Biodiversity Value

  • The open, nectar-bearing flowers attract small bees, butterflies and other short-tongued insects at a time of year when little else is in bloom.
  • Grown in containers it stays where it is planted and is not known to escape or spread into surrounding vegetation in India.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • One of the most widely traded flowering pot plants in the world, valued for its long bloom life, compact habit and very low water demand.
  • Sold in large numbers by Indian nurseries from winter through spring for balconies, offices, hotel interiors and as a gift plant.
  • Its succulent nature makes it well suited to water-wise indoor and balcony planting, needing a fraction of the irrigation of typical bedding flowers.
  • Also used in seasonal festival and event displays, where the dense colour holds up well under indoor lighting.

Medicinal Importance

  • Not used in mainstream Indian traditional medicine; this species is grown purely as an ornamental.
  • Like other members of the genus it contains bufadienolide compounds, which act on the heart and make the leaves and flowers unsafe to eat.
  • Regarded as toxic to cats, dogs and grazing animals if chewed, so pots are best kept out of reach of pets and small children.

Field Notes

  • Conservation Status: Not evaluated (NE) - IUCN Red List
  • Family Crassulaceae. Also sold as Flaming Katy, Florist Kalanchoe, Christmas Kalanchoe and Madagascar Widow's-thrill.
  • Named after Robert Blossfeld, the German plant breeder who introduced it to European horticulture in the 1930s.
