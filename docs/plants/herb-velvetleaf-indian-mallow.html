Velvetleaf Indian Mallow

Also known as: Velvetleaf, Indian Mallow, Butterprint, China-jute

KingdomPlantae
FamilyMalvaceae
GenusAbutilon
SpeciesAbutilon theophrasti
Scientific nameAbutilon theophrasti Medik.
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Abutilon is a large genus in the mallow family distributed throughout the tropics and subtropics of the Americas, Africa, Asia and Australia; herbage is generally hairy to woolly, with bell-shaped yellow, orange or reddish flowers. Abutilon theophrasti (velvetleaf) grows 0.5-3 m tall and is a common weed of disturbed and cultivated ground.

Biodiversity Value

  • Large hairy leaves and tall growth quickly shade out competing seedlings on disturbed farmland.
  • Yellow flowers attract bees; seeds can remain viable in soil for many years, aiding long-term persistence.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Several Abutilon species and hybrids are cultivated as ornamental garden plants (sometimes called flowering maple or parlor maple). Velvetleaf itself is more often regarded as a competitive agricultural weed.

Medicinal Importance

  • Leaves and seeds used in traditional Chinese medicine for fever and diarrhoea.
