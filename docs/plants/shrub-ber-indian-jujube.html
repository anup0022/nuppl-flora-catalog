Ber Indian Jujube

Also known as: Ber / Indian Jujube / Chinese Date

Scientific nameZiziphus mauritiana (most likely) or Ziziphus nummularia
TypeThorny, deciduous shrub to small tree
CategoryShrubs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to the Indian subcontinent, including UP. Very common across Kanpur in fields, wastelands, and roadsides
  • Thrives in hot, dry, arid and semi-arid climates. Extremely drought and heat tolerant
  • Grows in full sun. Common in open, degraded land and along field bunds
  • Prefers sandy-loam to clay soils. Tolerates poor, saline, and alkaline soils very well
  • Deep taproot makes it survive long dry spells. Commonly seen in rural UP
  • Naturalizes easily. The plant in your photo is growing wild among grass

Economic Importance

  • Fruit crop: Major fruit tree of dry regions. The small, round berries turn yellow to reddish when ripe. Eaten fresh, dried, or made into candy, chutney, and powder. Rich in vitamin C
  • Drought fruit: One of the few fruits that gives good yield in arid zones with minimal care. Important for farmer income in UP and Rajasthan
  • Prevents soil erosion. Deep roots stabilize bunds. Flowers are a good nectar source for bees

Medicinal Importance

  • In Ayurveda, fruits, leaves, bark, and roots used for indigestion, diarrhea, wounds, and as a blood purifier. Leaves have anti-inflammatory properties. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
  • Fodder: Leaves are nutritious fodder for goats, sheep, and camels. Lopped regularly in villages
  • Fencing: Thorny branches make excellent bio-fencing for fields to keep animals out
  • Timber & Fuel: Hard wood used for agricultural tools and as firewood
  • Lac cultivation: Major host plant for lac insect. Lac resin collected from Ber trees is economically important
