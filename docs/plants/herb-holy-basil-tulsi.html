Holy Basil (Tulsi)

Also known as: Holy Basil / Tulsi

KingdomPlantae
GenusOcimum
SpeciesOcimum tenuiflorum
Scientific nameOcimum tenuiflorum L. (Syn. Ocimum sanctum L.)
TypeHerb (Aromatic Shrub)
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Commonly grown in home gardens, temple premises, fields, and tropical to subtropical regions. It grows well in warm climates with well-drained soil.

Biodiversity Value

  • It is considered a sacred plant and is worshipped in many Indian households.
  • It attracts bees and other pollinators, helping maintain biodiversity.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Tulsi is an aromatic medicinal herb belonging to the family Lamiaceae.
  • It is widely used in Ayurveda for treating cough, cold, fever, and respiratory disorders.
  • The leaves are used to prepare herbal tea and traditional medicines.
  • Tulsi has antibacterial, antiviral, antifungal, and antioxidant properties.
  • It is considered a sacred plant and is worshipped in many Indian households.
  • The essential oil extracted from Tulsi is used in perfumes and herbal products.
  • It attracts bees and other pollinators, helping maintain biodiversity.
  • The plant is easy to grow and requires minimal care.
  • It helps improve air quality due to its aromatic compounds.
  • Tulsi has great medicinal, religious, ecological, and economic importance.

Medicinal Importance

  • Widely used in Ayurveda for cough, cold, fever and respiratory disorders; leaves are brewed as a medicinal herbal tea.
  • Has documented antibacterial, antiviral and antioxidant properties.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference

  • Tulsi holds a uniquely sacred place in Ayurveda; classical texts describe it as a Rasayana used for respiratory and general wellbeing, alongside its religious significance.
  • Shloka (commonly cited in Puranic/devotional tradition, not manuscript-verified here): "यत्र तुलसी तत्र गोविन्दो यत्र गोविन्दस्तत्र माधवः।" (yatra tulasī tatra govindo yatra govindas tatra mādhavaḥ) — "Where there is Tulsi, there is Govinda; where there is Govinda, there is Madhava."
