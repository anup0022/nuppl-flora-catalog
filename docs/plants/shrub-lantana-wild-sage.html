Lantana Wild Sage

Also known as: Lantana, Wild Sage, Ghaneri

KingdomPlantae
FamilyVerbenaceae
GenusLantana
SpeciesLantana camara
Scientific nameLantana camara L.
CategoryShrubs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to Central and South America; a perennial, erect sprawling or scandent shrub that typically grows to around 2 m tall, forming dense thickets, and can scramble up into trees to reach 6 m. Small tubular four-petalled flowers, in shades of red, yellow, white, pink and orange, are borne in terminal clusters that change colour with age after pollination. Now naturalized in around 60 tropical and subtropical countries, including large areas of India, where it is considered one of the world's worst invasive weeds, forming dense understorey thickets that suppress native vegetation regeneration.

Biodiversity Value

  • Small tubular four-petalled flowers, in shades of red, yellow, white, pink and orange, are borne in terminal clusters that change colour with age after pollination.
  • Widely grown historically as an ornamental garden plant for its long flowering season and attractiveness to butterflies, though it is now recognised in many regions as an aggressive invasive weed toxic to grazing livestock.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Widely grown historically as an ornamental garden plant for its long flowering season and attractiveness to butterflies, though it is now recognised in many regions as an aggressive invasive weed toxic to grazing livestock.

Medicinal Importance

  • Used in Indian traditional herbal medicine for skin itches, ulcers, chicken pox and asthma; studies have found antimicrobial, fungicidal and insecticidal properties in its leaves, though unripe green fruits and foliage are toxic if ingested by animals and possibly humans.
