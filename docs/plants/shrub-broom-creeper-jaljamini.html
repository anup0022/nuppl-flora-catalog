Broom Creeper ( Jaljamini)

Also known as: Broom Creeper / Jaljamini / Farid Buti / Patalgarudi

KingdomPlantae
GenusCocculus
SpeciesCocculus hirsutus
Scientific nameCocculus hirsutus
TypePerennial climbing/scandent shrub
CategoryShrubs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to India, Africa, and Southeast Asia. Very common weed across UP, including the Kanpur region where your photo was taken
  • Thrives in hot, dry to moderately moist tropical and subtropical climate. Grows year-round
  • Found in wastelands, open fields, roadsides, scrub jungles, and along fences. Tolerates full sun to partial shade
  • Grows in all soil types - sandy, loamy, rocky, or clay. Drought-tolerant and hardy. Doesn’t need care
  • Common in monsoon and post-monsoon season. Dies back in extreme summer but regrows from rootstock with rains

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Common early colonizer of disturbed land. Helps bind soil. Food plant for some butterfly larvae
  • No major commercial crop: Considered a weed in fields, but valued in traditional medicine systems

Medicinal Importance

  • Important in Ayurveda, Unani, and folk medicine. Root and leaves used for diabetes, urinary disorders, fever, skin diseases, and as a cooling agent. Leaf juice applied on eczema and prickly heat. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
  • Traditional uses: Crushed leaves produce a mucilaginous jelly used to cool the body in summer. Called "Jaljamini" because of this water-holding property
  • Fiber: Stems were traditionally used to make brooms - hence "Broom Creeper"
  • Fodder: Leaves browsed by goats and sheep, especially in dry regions
