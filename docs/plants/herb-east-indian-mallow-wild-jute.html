East Indian Mallow Wild Jute

Also known as: East Indian Mallow, Wild Jute

KingdomPlantae
FamilyMalvaceae
GenusCorchorus
SpeciesCorchorus aestuans
Scientific nameCorchorus aestuans L.
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • A member of the genus Corchorus (jute), which comprises around 40-100 species of tall annual herbs native to tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, reaching 2-4 m in height with alternate lanceolate leaves and small yellow five-petalled flowers. Corchorus aestuans is a common weed of disturbed ground, waysides and cultivated fields across India and other tropical regions.

Biodiversity Value

  • Small yellow flowers attract bees and other pollinating insects.
  • Fibrous stems and quick growth on disturbed soil help suppress bare ground and limit erosion.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Related jute species (Corchorus capsularis and C. olitorius) are the source of the jute fibre, the most widely cultivated vegetable fibre after cotton; the young leaves of several Corchorus species, including C. aestuans, are consumed as a leafy vegetable in parts of South Asia and Africa, with a mucilaginous texture similar to okra when cooked.

Medicinal Importance

  • Leaves used in some traditional medicine systems as a mild laxative and diuretic.
