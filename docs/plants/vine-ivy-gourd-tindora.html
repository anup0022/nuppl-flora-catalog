Vines / Climbers Specimen

Ivy Gourd Tindora

Also known as: Ivy Gourd, Tindora, Scarlet Gourd

KingdomPlantae
FamilyCucurbitaceae
GenusCoccinia
SpeciesCoccinia grandis
Scientific nameCoccinia grandis (L.) Voigt
CategoryVines / Climbers

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native range extends from Africa to Asia, including India, growing primarily in tropical climates. A perennial climbing vine with tendrils and glabrous, five-lobed leaves; introduced and often highly invasive in the Pacific Islands, Hawaii, Florida, Australia and Texas, where it can grow up to four inches a day and smother other vegetation.

Uses

  • The fruit and young shoots are a common part of Indian cuisine (fried, stuffed, pickled, or used in sambar) and are also eaten in Southeast Asian and Thai cuisines. Fruit is rich in beta-carotene.

Biodiversity Value

  • Known in Indian languages as bimba or bimbika, the fruit carries symbolic significance in Jain and Vaishnavite traditions.
  • Appears as a poetic metaphor for red lips in classical Sanskrit and Prakrit literature.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Fruit and shoots are a popular vegetable across South and Southeast Asian cuisines, giving it steady cultivation demand.
  • Fast climbing growth is useful as quick-covering fence and trellis planting, though it can turn invasive if unmanaged.
