Snapdragon Dog Flower

Also known as: Snapdragon / Dog Flower / Antirrhinum / Kutta Phool

Visual record01 photographs
Scientific nameAntirrhinum majus
TypeAnnual or perennial herb, family Plantaginaceae
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to the Mediterranean region and North Africa. Introduced as a garden ornamental and now widely cultivated across India as a winter annual. Very common in UP including the Kanpur area in your coordinatesThrives in cool to mild subtropical climate. Prefers 10-25°C for best growth and flowering. Grown in UP during winter and spring, Oct-Mar. Dies in summer heat above 35°CNeeds full sun in winter. Tolerates light shade but flowers poorly. Afternoon shade helps in late springPrefers well-drained, fertile loamy soil rich in organic matter. pH 6.2-7.0. Does not tolerate waterlogging or heavy clayPlanted in home gardens, parks, flower beds, and pots. Rarely naturalizes in India. Self-seeds in cool climates but not invasiveLife cycle: seeds sown Oct-Nov, flowers Dec-Mar, sets seed by April-May, then dies in summer heat

Economic Importance

  • Ornamental: Major winter-season garden flower in North India. Valued for tall spikes of colorful, dragon-mouth shaped flowers in white, yellow, pink, red, purple. Used in beds, borders, and as cut flowersCut flower: Popular in floristry for bouquets and vase arrangements. Good vase life of 7-10 daysCommercial floriculture: Sold as bedding plants and potted plants in nurseries from Nov-Feb. Important for winter landscapingChildren’s plant: Flowers can be gently squeezed to make the “dragon mouth” open and close – popular with kidsPollinator plant: Flowers attract bees and bumblebees. Good for pollinator gardens in winterNo major medicinal use: Mainly ornamental. Contains glycosides but not used in Indian traditional medicine. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use. Seeds are not edible
