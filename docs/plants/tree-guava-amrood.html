Guava Amrood

Also known as: Guava / Amrood

KingdomPlantae
GenusPsidium
SpeciesPsidium guajava
Scientific namePsidium guajava
TypeEvergreen to semi-deciduous shrub or small tree
CategoryTrees
IUCN Red ListLeast Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List LC

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to tropical America but naturalized across India. One of the most common fruit trees in UP, including Kanpur
  • Thrives in hot, tropical and subtropical climate. Tolerates both dry and humid conditions once established
  • Needs full sun for good fruiting. Grows in open areas, home gardens, orchards, and along field boundaries
  • Adapts to wide range of soils: sandy-loam, clay, even slightly saline or alkaline. Prefers well-drained soil but tolerates short waterlogging
  • Drought-hardy and low maintenance. Very common in village homes and farm bunds in North India
  • Flowers twice a year in UP - monsoon crop (rainy) and winter crop (mrig bahar)

Biodiversity Value

  • Also supports processing industry for pulp and nectar
  • Provides shade and attracts birds

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Fruit crop: Major commercial fruit in India. UP is a top producer. Eaten fresh, used in jellies, jams, juices, and fruit salads. “Allahabad Safeda” is a famous variety from UP
  • Nutritional: Excellent source of vitamin C - has 4-5x more than oranges. Also rich in fiber, vitamin A, potassium, and antioxidants like lycopene in pink varieties
  • Medicinal: Leaves, bark, and fruit used in Ayurveda for diarrhea, diabetes, cough, and oral health. Leaf decoction used as mouthwash. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
  • Livelihood: Millions of small farmers depend on guava orchards. Also supports processing industry for pulp and nectar
  • Wood: Hard, strong wood used for tool handles and engravings. Small branches used as firewood
  • Environmental: Hardly tree for wasteland reclamation. Provides shade and attracts birds
  • Leaves: Used as fodder in some areas. Leaf extract has antimicrobial properties

Medicinal Importance

  • Leaves, bark and fruit used in Ayurveda for diarrhoea, cough and oral health; leaf decoction used as a mouthwash.
