Gotu Kola

Also known as: Gotu Kola, Indian Pennywort, Asiatic Pennywort

KingdomPlantae
FamilyApiaceae
GenusCentella
SpeciesCentella asiatica
Scientific nameCentella asiatica (L.) Urban
CategoryHerbs
IUCN Red ListLeast Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List LC

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to tropical regions of Africa, Asia, Australia and islands of the western Pacific; a herbaceous perennial with slender creeping stolons, growing in temperate and tropical swampy areas, though it can also be cultivated in drier, regularly watered garden soil. Considered invasive in a number of Pacific islands.

Uses

  • Widely consumed as a leafy green vegetable across South and Southeast Asian cuisines - for example as thankuni pata in Bangladesh and West Bengal, gotu kola malluma in Sri Lanka, and pegaga in Malaysia - and used to prepare cooling drinks in Thailand, Cambodia and Vietnam.

Biodiversity Value

  • Creeping stolons form dense ground cover in moist soil, helping retain moisture and limit erosion along banks.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Widely eaten as a leafy vegetable and used in traditional medicine across South and Southeast Asia, with steady cultivation demand.

Medicinal Importance

  • Long used in traditional medicine for dermatological conditions and minor wounds, although clinical efficacy and safety have not been firmly established; adverse effects including contact dermatitis and, with prolonged use, liver disease have been reported in case studies and reviews.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference

  • Classical Ayurvedic name is Mandukaparni; described in traditional Nighantu texts as a Medhya Rasayana, a dravya associated with memory and mental clarity.
