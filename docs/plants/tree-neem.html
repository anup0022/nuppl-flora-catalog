Trees Specimen

Neem

KingdomPlantae
FamilyMeliaceae
GenusAzadirachta
SpeciesAzadirachta indica
Scientific nameAzadirachta indica
TypeEvergreen tree, grows up to 15–20 m.
CategoryTrees
IUCN Red ListLeast Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List LC

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to India; thrives in dry, arid, and tropical climates.
  • Pinnate leaves with serrated leaflets, bitter taste, small white flowers, and green drupes (neem fruits).

Biodiversity Value

  • Flowers attract bees; fruit is eaten by birds and bats, which disperse the seeds widely.
  • Natural pest-repellent compounds in its leaves lower local insect pressure, indirectly benefiting nearby plants.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Famous in Ayurveda and Unani medicine.
  • Leaves used for skin diseases, infections, and purification.
  • Neem oil extracted from seeds is a natural pesticide.
  • Bark used for dental care (neem datun).
  • Acts as a blood purifier in traditional medicine.
  • Widely used in cosmetics, soaps, shampoos.
  • Neem cake (seed residue) used as organic fertilizer.
  • Helps in mosquito and insect control.
  • Economically valuable in pharmaceutical and agricultural industries.
  • Culturally significant in India for rituals and health practices.

Medicinal Importance

  • Leaves, bark and oil are central to Ayurveda and Unani medicine for skin diseases, infections and as a blood purifier.
  • Neem oil is a natural pesticide and antiseptic; twigs traditionally used for dental care.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference

  • Classical Ayurvedic name is Nimba; documented in traditional texts as Krimighna (anti-parasitic) and Kushtaghna (used for skin disorders).
