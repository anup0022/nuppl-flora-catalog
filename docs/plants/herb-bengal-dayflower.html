Bengal Dayflower

Also known as: Bengal Dayflower, Tropical Spiderwort

KingdomPlantae
FamilyCommelinaceae
GenusCommelina
SpeciesCommelina benghalensis
Scientific nameCommelina benghalensis L.
CategoryHerbs
IUCN Red ListLeast Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List LC

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to tropical Asia and Africa; widely introduced to the neotropics, Hawaii, the West Indies and both coasts of North America. A perennial herb associated with disturbed soils such as yards, lawns and cultivated fields; considered a serious weed of cotton, tea, coffee, cassava and peanut crops across its native and introduced range, and placed on the US Federal Noxious Weed List.

Biodiversity Value

  • Distinctive blue flowers attract bees; mat-forming growth helps retain soil moisture on bare ground.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • A troublesome weed of cotton and tea crops, though young shoots are eaten as a leafy vegetable in parts of Asia and Africa.

Medicinal Importance

  • Used in China as a medicinal herb with reported diuretic, febrifuge and anti-inflammatory effects. In Pakistan it is used to treat skin swellings and leprosy and as a laxative; in Nepal the young leaves are eaten as a vegetable, a paste from the plant is used to treat burns, and root juice is used for indigestion.
