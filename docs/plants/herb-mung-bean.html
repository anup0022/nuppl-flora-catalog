Mung Bean

Also known as: Green Gram / Moong / Mung Bean

KingdomPlantae
GenusVigna
SpeciesVigna radiata
Scientific nameVigna radiata
TypeAnnual climbing herb
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to India and Southeast Asia. Major kharif crop across UP, including the Kanpur region
  • Thrives in hot, humid tropical and subtropical climate. Grows well at 25-35°C
  • Needs full sun. Short-day plant - flowering triggered by shorter day length in late monsoon
  • Prefers well-drained sandy-loam to loamy soil. Tolerates low fertility and slight alkalinity. Doesn’t tolerate waterlogging
  • Grown in fields, bunds, and as an intercrop. The photo shows a dense, healthy stand
  • Short duration crop of 60-75 days. Often sown with onset of monsoon in UP

Biodiversity Value

  • As a legume, its roots fix atmospheric nitrogen, naturally enriching soil for the next crop in rotation.
  • Flowers attract bees, and its short duration makes it a useful catch crop between main growing seasons.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Pulse crop: One of India’s most important pulses. UP is a major producer. Split green gram (moong dal) and whole moong are staple foods
  • Nutritional: Excellent source of plant protein, fiber, folate, iron, and magnesium. Easy to digest. Sprouted moong is rich in vitamins
  • Livelihood: Important cash crop for small farmers. Short duration lets farmers take another crop in the same year
  • Soil fertility: As a legume, it fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root nodules. Improves soil fertility for the next crop like wheat
  • Fodder: Green plants and husk after threshing used as nutritious cattle fodder
  • Medicinal: In Ayurveda, considered light, cooling, and easy to digest. Used in fever, detox diets, and for convalescents. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
  • Industrial: Starch from moong used in noodles and snacks. Flour used in papad, cheela, and halwa

Field Notes

  • Note: Could also be Black Gram / Urad if the flowers are yellow. Both look very similar at this vegetative stage.
