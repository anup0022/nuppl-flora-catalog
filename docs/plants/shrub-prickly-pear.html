Prickly Pear

Also known as: Prickly Pear, Nagphani

KingdomPlantae
FamilyCactaceae
GenusOpuntia
SpeciesOpuntia genus
Scientific nameOpuntia sp. (genus Opuntia Mill.)
CategoryShrubs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • A genus of cacti native to the Americas, domesticated in central Mexico some 8,000-9,000 years ago, and since introduced to Australia, southern Europe, the Middle East, parts of Africa and South Asia including India. Species typically form large, trunk-forming or shrubby succulents with flattened, spiny green pads (cladodes) and showy flowers; many species have become significant invasive weeds outside their native range, notably in Australia and South Africa.

Biodiversity Value

  • Flowers attract bees and other pollinators; ripe fruit is eaten by birds and small mammals, aiding seed dispersal.
  • Extreme drought tolerance lets it colonise degraded, rocky land, though it can become locally invasive.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • The fruit ("tuna" or prickly pear) and young pads (nopales) are widely eaten, especially in Mexican cuisine; also used for fodder, cochineal dye production (via the scale insect Dactylopius coccus), biofuel research and as living fences in arid regions.

Medicinal Importance

  • Used in Mexican folk medicine for wound healing and digestive or urinary tract inflammation, though robust clinical evidence for these uses remains limited.
