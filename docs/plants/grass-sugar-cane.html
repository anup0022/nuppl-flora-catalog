Grasses Specimen

Sugar cane

Also known as: Sugarcane / Ganna / Ikh

KingdomPlantae
GenusSaccharum
SpeciesSaccharum officinarum
Scientific nameSaccharum officinarum
TypePerennial giant grass
CategoryGrasses

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Native to New Guinea and Southeast Asia. India is the 2nd largest producer globally, and UP is the largest sugarcane-growing state
  • Thrives in hot, humid tropical and subtropical climate. Needs long, warm growing season of 10-12 months
  • Requires full sun and high rainfall or irrigation - about 1500-2500 mm water per crop
  • Grows best in deep, fertile, well-drained loamy to clay-loam soils rich in organic matter. Tolerates slightly saline soils
  • Widely cultivated in fields across UP, including Kanpur region. Planted in spring or autumn
  • Frost sensitive. Cold waves in North India can damage the crop, but it regrows from the base

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Sugar industry: Main source of sugar and jaggery (gur) in India. UP has the highest number of sugar mills. Backbone of rural economy in western and central UP
  • Employment: Provides livelihood to millions of farmers, mill workers, and transporters. Major cash crop for UP farmers
  • By-products:
  • Bagasse: Fibrous residue used as fuel in sugar mills, for paper, particle board, and electricity generation
  • Molasses: Used to produce ethanol, alcohol, rum, and as livestock feed. Key for India’s ethanol-blending program
  • Pressmud: Used as organic manure and for biogas
  • Juice: Fresh sugarcane juice is a popular summer drink. Rich in iron, calcium, and instant energy
  • Cultural: Used in festivals like Pongal and Makar Sankranti. Chewing raw cane is common in villages
  • Fodder: Tops and leaves used as green fodder for cattle after harvest

Medicinal Importance

  • In Ayurveda, juice is used for jaundice, urinary issues, and as a cooling tonic. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
