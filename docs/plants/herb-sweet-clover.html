Sweet Clover

Also known as: Sweet Clover, Melilot

KingdomPlantae
FamilyFabaceae
GenusMelilotus
SpeciesMelilotus indicus
Scientific nameMelilotus indicus (L.) All.
CategoryHerbs

Habitat & Growing Conditions

  • Melilotus is a genus of about 23 species of annual, biennial or perennial herbs native to Europe, Asia and Africa, closely related to true clovers (Trifolium). Melilotus indicus (Indian sweet clover, small melilot) is common on disturbed and cultivated ground across warm temperate and subtropical regions including India.

Biodiversity Value

  • As a legume, its roots fix nitrogen and improve soil fertility for surrounding plants.
  • Fragrant flowers are a good nectar source for bees, and it readily colonises disturbed roadside soils.

Economic & Ecological Importance

  • Grown as a green manure crop to improve soil nitrogen and organic matter, and used as forage; the plant's characteristic sweet hay-like smell comes from coumarin in its tissues, though mould-damaged or decaying material can develop the toxic anticoagulant dicoumarol, historically responsible for "sweet-clover disease" in cattle.

Medicinal Importance

  • Used in traditional Western herbalism for circulation and varicose veins, owing to its natural coumarin content.
