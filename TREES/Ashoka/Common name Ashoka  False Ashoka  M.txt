Common name: Ashoka / False Ashoka / Mast Tree  
Scientific name: Polyalthia longifolia 
Note: Not to be confused with Saraca asoca which is the "true Ashoka"
Family: Annonaceae

Habitat:
- Native to India and Sri Lanka
- Very common in UP including Kanpur area at your coordinates. Widely planted as avenue trees, in parks, campuses, and along roadsides
- Thrives in tropical to subtropical climate. Highly tolerant to pollution and drought
- Grows in almost any well-drained soil. Prefers full sun
- Tall, evergreen tree, 10-20 m tall. Distinctive columnar/ pyramidal shape. Leaves are long, narrow, lance-shaped with wavy margins and drooping habit like in your photo. Young leaves are coppery-red

Economic importance:
- Ornamental: Most popular avenue and roadside tree in Indian cities because of its narrow shape and dense foliage. Used for noise and dust reduction
- Medicinal: Bark and leaves used in traditional medicine for fever, skin diseases, and as an astringent. Has antimicrobial properties. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
- Other uses: Wood is light and used for making boxes, planks, and matches. Leaves used for decoration in rituals and festivals
- Ecological: Excellent for urban greening. Provides shade and absorbs air pollutants
