Common name: Jackfruit, Kathal
Scientific name: Artocarpus heterophyllus Lam.
Family: Moraceae

Habitat: An evergreen tree reaching 9-21 m tall, likely originating in the Western Ghats of India and now widely cultivated across South and Southeast Asia and tropical regions worldwide. Flowers and fruit are borne directly on the trunk and branches (cauliflory); the jackfruit is the largest tree-borne fruit, weighing up to 55 kg.

Economic Importance: A major food crop across South and Southeast Asia; unripe fruit is cooked as a savoury vegetable and meat substitute, ripe fruit is eaten fresh or in desserts, and the seeds are boiled or roasted and eaten. India is the world's leading producer. The termite-resistant golden timber is prized for furniture and musical instruments including the veena and mridangam.
