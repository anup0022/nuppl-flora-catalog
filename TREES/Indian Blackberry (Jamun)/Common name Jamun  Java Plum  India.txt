Common name: Jamun / Java Plum / Indian Blackberry / Jambul
Scientific name: Syzygium cumini (older name: Eugenia jambolana)
Type: Evergreen tree

Habitat:
- Native to the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia
- Widely cultivated across UP, including Kanpur. Common in gardens, farms, and along roads
- Thrives in hot, tropical to subtropical climate. Tolerates drought once established
- Grows in full sun. Prefers deep, loamy, well-drained soil but adapts to clay, sandy, and even saline soils
- Commonly found near villages, along field boundaries, and in orchards
- Can tolerate temporary waterlogging. Hardy tree for North Indian conditions

Economic importance:
- Fruit crop: Major summer fruit in India. The purple-black berries are eaten fresh, made into juice, jam, jelly, vinegar, and wine. Has a sweet-astringent taste
- Medicinal: Seeds, bark, leaves, and fruit used extensively in Ayurveda. Seeds are famous for anti-diabetic properties - contain jamboline which helps regulate blood sugar. Bark used for sore throat and ulcers. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
- Nutritional: Fruit is rich in vitamin C, iron, potassium, and antioxidants. Low in calories
- Timber: Hard, durable wood is water-resistant. Used for railway sleepers, agricultural implements, and construction
- Cultural: Sacred tree in Hinduism and Buddhism. Associated with Lord Krishna and Lord Ganesha
- Ecological: Dense canopy provides shade. Flowers attract bees. Fruits feed birds, bats, and monkeys
- Other uses: Leaves used as fodder. Bark yields tannins for dyeing and tanning leather
