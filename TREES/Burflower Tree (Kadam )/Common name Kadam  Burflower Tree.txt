Common name: Kadam / Burflower Tree / Kadamba
Scientific name: Neolamarckia cadamba (older name: Anthocephalus cadamba)
Type: Deciduous to semi-evergreen tree

Habitat:
- Native to South and Southeast Asia, including India
- Very common across UP and planted widely in Kanpur for shade and ornament
- Grows naturally in moist, warm areas near rivers and in mixed forests
- Thrives in hot, humid climate. Fast-growing in full sun
- Prefers deep, rich, well-drained alluvial soil but adapts to varied soils
- Commonly planted in parks, along roads, and in gardens. Your photo shows it near a building
- Sheds leaves in dry season in North India, flushes new bright green leaves before monsoon

Economic importance:
- Timber: Lightweight, soft wood used for plywood, packing cases, matches, and paper pulp. Not termite resistant
- Ornamental & Avenue tree: Planted for fast shade and large, glossy leaves. The round, orange, fragrant flowers are iconic in monsoon
- Cultural & Religious: Sacred to Lord Krishna. Associated with monsoon and Radha-Krishna legends. Flowers used in worship
- Medicinal: Bark, leaves, and fruits used in Ayurveda for fever, cough, wounds, and skin diseases. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
- Ecological: Fast-growing tree for afforestation. Flowers attract bees. Fruits eaten by birds and bats
- Perfumery: Flowers yield essential oil used in attar and perfumes
