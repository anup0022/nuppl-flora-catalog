Common name: Rubber Plant, Rubber Fig, Indian Rubber Tree
Scientific name: Ficus elastica Roxb. ex Hornem.
Family: Moraceae

Habitat: A large banyan-group fig native to eastern South and Southeast Asia (Nepal, Bhutan, Northeastern India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Yunnan and Malaysia), growing to 30-40 m tall in the wild with a stout trunk, aerial and buttressing roots, and broad glossy oval leaves. In parts of Meghalaya, India, its aerial roots are trained by local communities to form living root bridges over rivers.

Economic Importance: Historically an important source of natural latex rubber in Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula before being superseded by the Para rubber tree (Hevea brasiliensis) in the early 1900s; today grown worldwide as a popular ornamental and houseplant, with numerous variegated cultivars.

Medicinal Importance: A decoction of the aerial rootlets has traditional use for wound healing, and young leaf tips are occasionally used as salad greens; however, the plant's latex can irritate skin and eyes and is toxic if ingested.
