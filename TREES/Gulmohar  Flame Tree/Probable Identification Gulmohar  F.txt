Probable Identification: Gulmohar / Flame Tree

Common Name: Gulmohar
Scientific Name: Delonix regia (Bojer ex Hook.) Raf.
Conservation Status: Least Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List
Plant Type: Tree
Habitat: Commonly planted along roadsides, parks, gardens, campuses, and urban areas. It grows well in tropical and subtropical climates with well-drained soil.

Biodiversity Value:
- Its roots help bind the soil and reduce soil erosion.
- The leaves and flowers provide shelter and food for birds and insects.

Economic Importance (8–10 lines)
Gulmohar is a large deciduous ornamental tree belonging to the family Fabaceae.
It is famous for its bright red-orange flowers that bloom during summer.
The tree provides excellent shade because of its broad umbrella-shaped canopy.
It is widely planted for roadside and landscape beautification.
It helps improve air quality by absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen.
Its roots help bind the soil and reduce soil erosion.
The leaves and flowers provide shelter and food for birds and insects.
The wood is sometimes used for making small wooden articles and fuel.
It is drought-tolerant once established and requires relatively low maintenance.
Gulmohar has high ecological, ornamental, and environmental importance.

Medicinal Importance:
- Bark and leaves used in some folk medicine traditions for rheumatism and as an anti-inflammatory.
