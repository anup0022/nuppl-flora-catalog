Common name: Saptaparni / Devil Tree
Scientific name: Alstonia scholaris
Family: Apocynaceae
Type: Evergreen tree
Conservation Status: Least Concern (LC) - IUCN Red List
Habitat: Native to South and Southeast Asia; grows in moist tropical forests, roadsides, gardens, and institutional grounds. Tolerates warm lowland conditions and well-drained soil.
Features: Tall straight trunk, whorled branches, glossy oblong leaves, milky latex, and fragrant greenish-white flowers in clusters.
Biodiversity Value: Flowers provide nectar for insects and the dense crown offers nesting cover for birds.
Economic importance: Planted as a shade and avenue tree; wood is used for light construction, boxes, and plywood.
Medicinal importance: Bark and leaves are used in traditional medicine, but medicinal use should be supervised by a qualified professional.
