Common name: Indian Bay Leaf, Tejpat, Malabar Leaf
Scientific name: Cinnamomum tamala (Buch.-Ham.) T.Nees & C.H.Eberm.
Family: Lauraceae

Habitat: A tree native to northern India (Assam and the Western Himalayas), Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar, Laos, Vietnam and southwestern China, growing in Himalayan foothill forests.

Economic Importance: The aromatic leaves, called tejpat or tejpatta, are a staple spice in Indian, Nepali and Bangladeshi cuisine, imparting a strong cassia-like aroma quite distinct from Mediterranean bay laurel, with which it is often confused and mislabelled; historically thought to be a major source of the classical spice "malabathrum".

Medicinal Importance: Leaves contain aromatic compounds including linalool and eugenol, and extracts have been studied for antidiarrhoeal, antifungal and antioxidant activity in traditional and experimental medicine.

Biodiversity Value:
- Aromatic foliage is browsed by few animals, giving the tree some protection from grazing pressure.
- Part of the mixed Himalayan foothill canopy that helps stabilise slopes and moderate microclimate.
