Common Name: Papaya

Scientific Name: Carica papaya

Family: Caricaceae

🌱 Growth Form & Habitat
Type: Fast‑growing, short‑lived tree (technically a giant herb, not a woody tree).

Habitat: Tropical and subtropical climates; thrives in well‑drained soil with sunlight.

Features: Hollow stem, large lobed leaves, produces sweet fruits.

💡 Economic & Medicinal Importance (8–10 lines)
Papaya fruit is eaten fresh and used in juices, jams, and desserts.

Rich in vitamin A, C, and antioxidants.

Green (unripe) papaya used in cooking and pickles.

Papain enzyme from latex aids digestion and is used in meat tenderizers.

Leaves used in traditional medicine for dengue fever and boosting platelets.

Seeds have antiparasitic properties.

Cultivated widely as a cash crop in tropical regions.

Provides nutritional food security in rural areas.

Plays a role in cosmetics (papaya extracts in soaps, creams).

Economically important for farmers due to high demand and quick yield.

Biodiversity Value:
- Flowers are pollinated mainly by moths and bees; ripe fruit is eaten and dispersed by birds and bats.
- Fast growth and continuous fruiting make it a reliable year-round food source in home gardens.
