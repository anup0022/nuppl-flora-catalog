Common name: Weeping Fig, Benjamin Fig
Scientific name: Ficus benjamina L.
Family: Moraceae

Habitat: Native to South and Southeast Asia and Australia, reaching up to 30 m tall in the wild with gracefully drooping branchlets and glossy oval leaves; grown widely as a shade and avenue tree in tropical and subtropical regions, and as a popular houseplant in temperate climates due to its tolerance of low light and poor growing conditions.

Economic Importance: One of the most common ornamental and indoor foliage trees worldwide, planted in parks, along roads and as bonsai; capable of some indoor air purification of gaseous formaldehyde, though its invasive root system can damage pavements and foundations.

Medicinal Importance: Leaves have been studied for antioxidant properties; however, the plant's latex is a recognised indoor allergen (the third most common after dust and pets) and can cause skin and respiratory reactions, particularly in individuals with latex sensitivity.
