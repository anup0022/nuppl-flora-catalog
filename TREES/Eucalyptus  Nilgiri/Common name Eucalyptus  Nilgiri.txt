Common name: Eucalyptus, Nilgiri, Gum Tree
Scientific name: Eucalyptus sp. (genus Eucalyptus L'Her.)
Family: Myrtaceae

Habitat: A genus of over 700 species, most native to Australia, with a small number native to New Guinea, Indonesia and the Philippines; introduced widely across the tropical and temperate world, including India, since the 18th and 19th centuries. Trees and mallees typically have oil-gland-covered leaves, distinctive flowers with a cap-like operculum covering the stamens in bud, and woody capsule fruits ("gumnuts").

Economic Importance: One of the most important plantation tree genera globally, grown for pulpwood and paper manufacture, timber, firewood, charcoal and eucalyptus oil (used in cleaning, antiseptics, decongestants and insect repellents); also planted historically in India and other countries to drain swampy ground and reduce malaria risk, though its high water demand and allelopathic effect on neighbouring plants have made some plantations controversial.

Medicinal Importance: Eucalyptus oil, steam-distilled from the leaves, has long been used as an antiseptic, decongestant and in aromatherapy; oil-rich leaves are also highly flammable, making eucalyptus plantations a notable fire risk in dry climates.
