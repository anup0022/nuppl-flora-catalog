Common name: Radha Ballabh, Akashbel, Giant Dodder, Ulan Ulan
Scientific name: Cuscuta reflexa Roxb.
Family: Convolvulaceae

Habitat: Common in the Indian subcontinent and the Greater Himalayas, extending as far south as Malaysia and Indonesia. A leafless, twining, parasitic vine that sprawls over a host plant, including large trees, forming garlands hanging from tree canopies as long as 10 metres; produces small, bell-shaped white flowers with yellow filaments. Because it lacks leaves and chlorophyll of its own, it draws water and nutrients entirely from its host plant.

Economic & Ecological Importance:
- A leafless parasitic vine with no direct commercial value; heavy infestations can weaken or kill host trees.
- Used in traditional medicine in some regions despite being generally regarded as a pest on cultivated trees.
