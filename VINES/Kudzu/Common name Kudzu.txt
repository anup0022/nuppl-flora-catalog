Common name: Kudzu
Scientific name: Pueraria montana (Lour.) Merr.
Family: Fabaceae

Habitat: Native to East Asia (China, Japan, Korea) and also found in India, Myanmar, Indochina, Thailand, Malaysia, the Pacific Islands and northern Australia. A perennial climbing vine with tuberous roots and rope-like stems up to 20 m long, capable of growing up to 20 m per year; prefers forest regrowth and sunny edge habitats but tolerates full sun or partial shade.

Economic Importance: Farmed and selectively bred by humans for millennia for food (starchy roots used since before 540 CE, sometimes sustaining people through famines), fibre (kudzu cloth, a major Chinese textile material for centuries), medicine, paper and basketry, and as livestock fodder. Kudzu starch is used in Japan as a thickening and gelling agent and in confectionery. Highly invasive where introduced outside its native range, particularly in the southeastern United States, where it is sometimes called "the vine that ate the South".

Medicinal Importance: Considered an important medicinal plant in traditional use and is sometimes sold as a dietary supplement.
