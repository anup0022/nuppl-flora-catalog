Common name: Rangoon Creeper / Madhumalti / Burma Creeper
Scientific name: Combretum indicum (older name: Quisqualis indica)
Type: Perennial woody vine or scandent shrub

Habitat:
- Native to tropical Asia, from India to Philippines and Malaysia
- Very common in gardens across India, including Kanpur and UP
- Thrives in full sun with well-drained, fertile soil
- Loves hot, humid climates. Flowers best in summer and monsoon
- Grows vigorously on fences, walls, arches, and pergolas
- Drought tolerant once established but needs regular water for best flowering

Biodiversity Value:

Economic importance:
- Ornamental: Extremely popular for its fragrant flowers that change color from white to pink to red. Used to cover walls, gates, and trellises
- Fragrance: Flowers are intensely sweet-scented, especially at night. Used in garlands
- Medicinal: In Ayurveda and folk medicine, seeds used as an anthelmintic to treat intestinal worms. Leaves and roots used for pain, fever, and skin issues. Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
- Commercial: Widely sold in nurseries as a fast-growing flowering climber
- Cultural: Common in Indian households. Name “Madhumalti” means “honey jasmine”
