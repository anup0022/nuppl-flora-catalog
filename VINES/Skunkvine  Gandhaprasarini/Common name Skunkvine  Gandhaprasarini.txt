Common name: Skunkvine, Gandhaprasarini, Stinkvine, Chinese Fever Vine
Scientific name: Paederia foetida L.
Family: Rubiaceae

Habitat: Native to temperate and tropical Asia, including Bangladesh, Bhutan, Cambodia, China and India; naturalized in the Mascarenes, Melanesia, Polynesia and Hawaii. Known for the strong sulphurous odour released when its leaves or stems are crushed or bruised, caused by sulphur compounds including dimethyl disulphide in the leaf oil.

Uses: Sometimes planted as an ornamental. Used as a culinary spice in traditional cooking of northeastern and eastern India; in Hainanese cuisine the leaves are ground into flour and mixed with rice for a sweet noodle soup.

Medicinal Importance: Has a recognized place in folk medicine across its native range, though specific claimed effects vary by region.
