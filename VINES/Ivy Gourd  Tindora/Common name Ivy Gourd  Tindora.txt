Common name: Ivy Gourd, Tindora, Scarlet Gourd
Scientific name: Coccinia grandis (L.) Voigt
Family: Cucurbitaceae

Habitat: Native range extends from Africa to Asia, including India, growing primarily in tropical climates. A perennial climbing vine with tendrils and glabrous, five-lobed leaves; introduced and often highly invasive in the Pacific Islands, Hawaii, Florida, Australia and Texas, where it can grow up to four inches a day and smother other vegetation.

Uses: The fruit and young shoots are a common part of Indian cuisine (fried, stuffed, pickled, or used in sambar) and are also eaten in Southeast Asian and Thai cuisines. Fruit is rich in beta-carotene.

Biodiversity Value:
- Known in Indian languages as bimba or bimbika, the fruit carries symbolic significance in Jain and Vaishnavite traditions.
- Appears as a poetic metaphor for red lips in classical Sanskrit and Prakrit literature.
