Common name: Sponge Gourd, Egyptian Cucumber, Vietnamese Luffa
Scientific name: Luffa aegyptiaca Mill. (synonym: Luffa cylindrica)
Family: Cucurbitaceae

Habitat: Native to South and Southeast Asia. An annual vine cultivated for its fruit; requires plenty of heat and water, though it can also be grown in temperate climates with a trellis for its tendrils to climb.

Uses: The young fruit is eaten as a vegetable across tropical Asia; young shoots, flowers and leaves can be cooked, and mature seeds can be roasted. The fully ripened, fibrous fruit is used to make scrubbing bath sponges (loofah/luffa sponges), and an edible oil can be extracted from the seeds, with the oil meal used to feed rabbits and catfish or as fertilizer. Also grown as an ornamental for its bright yellow flowers, and used as a gently abrasive natural exfoliant in the cosmetics industry.

Biodiversity Value:
- Yellow flowers attract bees and other pollinators; the vigorous climbing vine provides quick trellis cover.

Economic & Ecological Importance:
- Fruit is eaten as a vegetable across tropical Asia, and mature fibrous fruit is processed into loofah sponges and seed oil.
