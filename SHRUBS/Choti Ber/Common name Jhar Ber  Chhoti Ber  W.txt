Common name: Jhar Ber / Chhoti Ber / Wild Jujube / Malla  
Scientific name: Ziziphus nummularia  
Family: Rhamnaceae

Habitat:
- Native to India, Pakistan, and West Asia. Common across dry regions of India
- Very common in UP including Kanpur area in your coordinates. Found in wastelands, scrub forests, grazing lands, and field boundaries
- Thrives in tropical to subtropical arid and semi-arid climate. Highly drought and heat tolerant. Grows best at 25-40°C
- Needs full sun. Grows in poor, rocky, sandy, and saline soils. Does not like waterlogging
- Deciduous. Sheds leaves in extreme summer. New leaves are bright green like in your photo

Economic importance:
- Fruit: Small round berries "ber" are eaten fresh or dried. Sour-sweet taste. Rich in vitamin C, iron, and calcium. Used to make chutney, candy, and pickle
- Medicinal: Leaves, bark, root, and fruit used in Ayurveda for wounds, diarrhea, skin diseases, and fever. Has anti-inflammatory and anti-microbial properties. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
- Fodder: Leaves are excellent fodder for goats, sheep, and camels especially in dry season
- Fuel & Timber: Wood is hard and used as firewood and for making agricultural tools
- Ecological: Great for soil conservation, windbreaks, and wasteland reclamation. Fixes soil and provides shelter for birds. Spines make good live fencing for fields
- Other: Leaves used to feed lac insects. Bark yields tannin used in leather tanning
