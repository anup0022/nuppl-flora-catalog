Common name: Devil’s Backbone / Zigzag Plant / Slipper Flower / Naagdon
Scientific name: Euphorbia tithymaloides (older name: Pedilanthus tithymaloides)
Type: Perennial succulent shrub

Habitat:
- Native to tropical Americas, from Florida to Venezuela
- Widely grown across India as a garden and potted plant. Does very well in Kanpur’s climate
- Prefers full sun to partial shade. Needs well-drained, sandy or loamy soil
- Extremely drought tolerant. Hates waterlogging and frost
- Often used as a hedge or border plant in hot, dry regions

Economic importance:
- Ornamental: Popular for its unique zigzag stems and bright green foliage. Variegated varieties are common in landscaping
- Hedge plant: Dense growth makes it useful for boundary hedges and living fences
- Medicinal: In folk medicine, latex is used for warts, skin issues, and earache. Leaves used for inflammation. Important: The milky sap is toxic and irritating to skin and eyes. Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
- Cultural: Called “Naagdon” in India. Believed to keep snakes away, though not proven
- Commercial: Sold widely in nurseries as a low-maintenance succulent shrub

Biodiversity Value:
- Milky latex sap deters grazing animals and insect pests.
- Succulent, water-storing stems let it survive long dry spells with minimal care.

Medicinal Importance:
- Latex used in folk medicine for warts and skin issues, though the milky sap is toxic and irritating and should be handled with care.
