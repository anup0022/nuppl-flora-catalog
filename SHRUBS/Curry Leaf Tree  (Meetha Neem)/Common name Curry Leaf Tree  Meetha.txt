Common name: Curry Leaf Tree / Meetha Neem / Kadi Patta / Kariveppilai
Scientific name: Murraya koenigii
Type: Aromatic shrub to small tree, family RutaceaeHabitat:Native to India and Sri Lanka. Grows wild in foothills of the Himalayas, Western Ghats, and across tropical-subtropical India. Very common in UP including the Kanpur area in your coordinatesThrives in hot, humid tropical to subtropical climate. Loves UP summers at 25-40°C. Sensitive to frost. Leaves drop in severe winter but plant survives and flushes back in springNeeds full sun to partial shade for best growth and aroma – minimum 4-6 hours daily. Grows in shade but leaves have weaker flavorPrefers well-drained, fertile loamy soil rich in organic matter. pH 6.0-7.0. Tolerates slightly alkaline soil but not waterlogging or heavy clayPlanted in home gardens, farm boundaries, and pots. Also grows wild along roadsides and in open forests. Self-seeds from bird-dropped seedsEconomic importance:Culinary: Main use. Fresh leaves are essential in Indian cooking – tadka for dal, curries, chutneys, upma, poha. Distinct aroma comes from essential oils. Leaves sold fresh in all Indian markets. Dried powder also marketedMedicinal: Used in Ayurveda and folk medicine. Leaves said to help with diabetes, cholesterol, digestion, hair health, and anemia. Contains carbazole alkaloids, antioxidants, and iron. Note: Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal useCommercial crop: Cultivated commercially in South India, especially Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Karnataka. Coimbatore is a major market. Leaves harvested every 2-3 months once establishedCosmetic: Leaf extract used in hair oils and shampoos for preventing graying and hair fall. “Kadi patta hair oil” is popularEcological: Host plant for Common Mormon and Lime Butterfly caterpillars. Flowers attract beesOther: Stems used as datun for teeth cleaning in villages. Wood is hard, used for tool handles
