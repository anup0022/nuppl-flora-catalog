Common name: Henna, Mehndi, Egyptian Privet
Scientific name: Lawsonia inermis L.
Family: Lythraceae

Habitat: Native to northern and eastern Africa, Asia and northern Australia in semi-arid and tropical zones; a tall, glabrous, multi-branched shrub or small tree 1.8-7.6 m high with spine-tipped branchlets and elliptical, lanceolate leaves. Produces the most dye when grown at 35-45 degrees C and cannot tolerate temperatures below about 5 degrees C.

Economic Importance: The dried leaves are the source of the traditional henna dye used across South Asia, the Middle East and North Africa to colour skin, hair, nails, silk, wool and leather; commercially cultivated on a large scale for this purpose.

Medicinal Importance: Contains the compound lawsone, and preliminary studies suggest antioxidant, antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties, though clinical safety and efficacy in humans remain under study; henna is known to pose a risk to individuals with G6PD deficiency.

Biodiversity Value:
- Fragrant flowers attract bees and moths; thorny branches offer safe nesting cover for small birds.
- Deep roots and dense branching help stabilise soil in semi-arid hedgerows.
