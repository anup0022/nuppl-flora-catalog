Common name: Wild Caper Bush, Hedge Caper
Scientific name: Capparis sepiaria L.
Conservation Status: Least Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List
Family: Capparaceae

Habitat: A prickly, evergreen shrub growing 3-5 m tall with white flowers appearing from February onwards, found across dry deciduous forests, foothills and scrub jungles with a pantropical distribution spanning Africa, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, India and Australia; recorded in numerous Indian regions including Kolhapur, Dharwad, Mysore and Kerala.

Biodiversity Value:
- An important dry-season fodder source for wildlife such as chital, sambar and nilgai in Rajasthan; its bark serves as a larval host plant for the great orange-tip and yellow orange-tip butterflies of South India.

Economic Importance: An important dry-season fodder source for wildlife such as chital, sambar and nilgai in Rajasthan; its bark serves as a larval host plant for the great orange-tip and yellow orange-tip butterflies of South India.

Medicinal Importance: Flowers and fruit of this and related Capparis species have traditional use as food and folk medicine, including as a cooling agent and in oral health remedies documented among tribal communities of Rajasthan and Karnataka.
