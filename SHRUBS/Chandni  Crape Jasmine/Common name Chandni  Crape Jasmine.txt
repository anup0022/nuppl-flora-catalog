Common name: Chandni / Crape Jasmine / Pinwheel Flower / Tagar
Scientific name: Tabernaemontana divaricata (also called Ervatamia coronaria)
Conservation Status: Least Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List
Type: Evergreen shrub

Habitat:
- Native to India and Southeast Asia. Very common across UP, including Kanpur, as a garden and hedge plant
- Thrives in hot, humid tropical and subtropical climate. Grows well in North Indian summers and monsoon
- Prefers partial shade to full sun. Grows best in semi-shade in hot areas like UP
- Needs well-drained, fertile, slightly acidic to neutral soil rich in organic matter
- Commonly planted in gardens, parks, temples, and as hedges. Rarely found wild in your area - mostly cultivated
- Sensitive to frost. Needs protection in severe UP winters but regrows in spring

Biodiversity Value:
- Flowers offered in temples and used for worship.
- Flowers attract bees and butterflies.

Economic importance:
- Ornamental: One of the most popular flowering shrubs in India. Planted for glossy leaves and fragrant, white pinwheel flowers that bloom almost year-round
- Religious & Cultural: Flowers offered in temples and used for worship. Associated with purity. Common in garlands
- Medicinal: Roots, leaves, and flowers used in Ayurveda and folk medicine for toothache, inflammation, eye diseases, and skin problems. Contains alkaloids like coronaridine. Note: Plant is mildly toxic if ingested. Consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use
- Hedge plant: Dense foliage and compact growth make it ideal for hedges and borders in landscaping
- Perfumery: Flowers have a mild, sweet fragrance used in some traditional attars

Medicinal Importance:
- Roots, leaves and flowers used in Ayurveda and folk medicine for toothache, inflammation and eye diseases.
- The plant is mildly toxic if ingested and used only in prepared traditional remedies.
