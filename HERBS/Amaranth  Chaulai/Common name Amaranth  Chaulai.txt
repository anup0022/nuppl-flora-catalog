Common name: Amaranth, Chaulai
Scientific name: Amaranthus sp. (genus Amaranthus L.)
Family: Amaranthaceae

Habitat: A genus of about 75 species of herbaceous annual and perennial plants with a cosmopolitan distribution across tropical regions, found from lowlands to Himalayan elevations. Plants grow 1-2.5 m tall with a succulent, often reddish stem and dense catkin-like flower clusters.

Economic Importance: Widely cultivated worldwide as a leaf vegetable (called saag or cheera in India) and as a grain crop (amaranth grain, a gluten-free pseudocereal rich in protein, calcium, magnesium and dietary fibre). Ornamental species such as A. caudatus (love-lies-bleeding) are also grown for their long-lasting flower spikes.

Biodiversity Value:
- Seeds are eaten by birds; tall flower spikes offer good pollen for bees and other insects.
- Fast-growing leafy cover on disturbed ground helps prevent bare soil from washing away.
