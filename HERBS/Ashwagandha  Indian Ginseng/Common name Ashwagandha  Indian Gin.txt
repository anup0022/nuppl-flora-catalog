Common name: Ashwagandha / Indian Ginseng / Winter Cherry / Asgandh
Scientific name: Withania somnifera
Type: Perennial undershrub, family Solanaceae

Habitat: Native to India, North Africa, and the Middle East. Grows wild and cultivated across drier parts of India, very common in UP, MP, Rajasthan, and Gujarat. Thrives in hot, dry subtropical climate at 25-40°C, tolerating drought well but sensitive to frost and waterlogging. Needs full sun and well-drained, sandy loam to light red soil, growing well even in marginal, poor and alkaline soils (pH 7.5-8.0), though it dislikes heavy clay and standing water. Found wild on wastelands, field bunds, and roadsides; commercially cultivated as a rabi crop in dry regions, sown Oct-Nov and harvested Mar-Apr.

Economic Importance:
- Commercial crop: Major medicinal plant crop in India. MP's Neemuch and Mandsaur are the biggest producers; roots are graded and sold at Rs 150-400/kg and exported globally for herbal supplements.
- Ayurvedic industry: Key ingredient in Chyawanprash, Ashwagandharishta, and numerous proprietary medicines by companies such as Dabur, Himalaya and Patanjali.
- Other uses: Leaves applied on swelling and wounds. Berries used to coagulate milk for cheese-making in some regions. Seeds are diuretic.

Medicinal Importance: Roots, leaves, and berries used in Ayurveda, Unani, and Siddha for 3000+ years; classified as a Rasayana (rejuvenator) and used as an adaptogen for stress, anxiety, insomnia, and fatigue, giving it the market name "Indian Ginseng". Roots contain withanolides, alkaloids, and steroidal lactones, used in formulations for arthritis, immunity, strength, male fertility, and as a general tonic. Not recommended for pregnant women; consult a healthcare professional before any medicinal use.
