Common name: East Indian Mallow, Wild Jute
Scientific name: Corchorus aestuans L.
Family: Malvaceae

Habitat: A member of the genus Corchorus (jute), which comprises around 40-100 species of tall annual herbs native to tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, reaching 2-4 m in height with alternate lanceolate leaves and small yellow five-petalled flowers. Corchorus aestuans is a common weed of disturbed ground, waysides and cultivated fields across India and other tropical regions.

Economic Importance: Related jute species (Corchorus capsularis and C. olitorius) are the source of the jute fibre, the most widely cultivated vegetable fibre after cotton; the young leaves of several Corchorus species, including C. aestuans, are consumed as a leafy vegetable in parts of South Asia and Africa, with a mucilaginous texture similar to okra when cooked.

Biodiversity Value:
- Small yellow flowers attract bees and other pollinating insects.
- Fibrous stems and quick growth on disturbed soil help suppress bare ground and limit erosion.
