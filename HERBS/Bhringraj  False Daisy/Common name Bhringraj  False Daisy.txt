Common name: Bhringraj, False Daisy, Trailing Eclipta
Scientific name: Eclipta prostrata (L.) L.
Conservation Status: Least Concern (LC) — IUCN Red List
Family: Asteraceae

Habitat: Widespread across much of the world in moist places in warm temperate to tropical regions, including marsh edges, lake shores, river banks and brackish marshes. A hairy, cylindrical-stemmed herb with small white flower heads about 6-8 mm across.

Medicinal Importance: Known in Hindi-speaking regions of India as bhangra or bhringaraj, it has extensive traditional use in Ayurveda. In Southeast Asia the dried whole plant is used in traditional medicine, and leaves are extracted as a black hair dye and used in tattooing; the plant contains coumestans, polyacetylenes and flavonoids.

Biodiversity Value:
- Small white flower heads attract bees and hoverflies; thrives in damp soil, helping retain moisture.

Economic & Ecological Importance:
- Widely used in traditional hair-care oils and Ayurvedic formulations, sustaining demand as a cultivated herb.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference:
- Bhringaraja is the classical Ayurvedic name; documented in Bhavaprakasha Nighantu as a Keshya (hair-nourishing) dravya and a key ingredient in traditional hair oils.
