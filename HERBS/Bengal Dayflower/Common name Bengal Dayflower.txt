Common name: Bengal Dayflower, Tropical Spiderwort
Scientific name: Commelina benghalensis L.
Family: Commelinaceae

Habitat: Native to tropical Asia and Africa; widely introduced to the neotropics, Hawaii, the West Indies and both coasts of North America. A perennial herb associated with disturbed soils such as yards, lawns and cultivated fields; considered a serious weed of cotton, tea, coffee, cassava and peanut crops across its native and introduced range, and placed on the US Federal Noxious Weed List.

Medicinal Importance: Used in China as a medicinal herb with reported diuretic, febrifuge and anti-inflammatory effects. In Pakistan it is used to treat skin swellings and leprosy and as a laxative; in Nepal the young leaves are eaten as a vegetable, a paste from the plant is used to treat burns, and root juice is used for indigestion.
