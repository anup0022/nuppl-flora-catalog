Common name: Thorn Apple / Jimsonweed / Dhatura / Safed Dhatura
Scientific name: Datura stramonium
Type: Annual herb, family Solanaceae

Habitat: Native to North America, now naturalized as a cosmopolitan weed found across India in wastelands, roadsides, and disturbed areas. Thrives in hot, dry tropical to warm temperate climate, growing aggressively in summer and monsoon at 20-38°C but killed by frost. Needs full sun and well-drained, sandy to loamy soil; tolerates poor, nutrient-deficient, and alkaline soils but not waterlogging. Life cycle of 4-6 months: germinates with monsoon, flowers Aug-Oct, sets seed by Nov-Dec, then dies in winter.

Biodiversity Value:
- Offered to Lord Shiva in Hindu worship, especially during Shravan and Mahashivratri.
- White trumpet flowers open at night and are pollinated by moths; thorny seed pods deter animals.

Economic Importance:
- Commercial: Leaves and seeds collected for pharmaceutical companies; India exports Datura extract.
- Religious: Offered to Lord Shiva in Hindu worship, especially during Shravan and Mahashivratri.
- Toxic weed: All parts (leaves, flowers, seeds) are poisonous to humans and livestock; accidental poisoning is common in children due to the attractive flowers and spiny fruits, and it has no fodder value.

Medicinal Importance: A major source of the tropane alkaloids atropine, hyoscyamine, and scopolamine, used in modern medicine for asthma inhalers, pre-anaesthetic medication, motion sickness patches, and to dilate pupils. In Ayurveda, used only in very controlled, processed forms (as Dhatura, after shodhana purification) for asthma, pain, and skin diseases; the unprocessed plant is poisonous. All parts are highly toxic and self-medication is dangerous and can be fatal; this plant is for pharmaceutical industry use only under expert guidance.
