Common name: Philodendron
Scientific name: Philodendron sp. (genus Philodendron Schott)
Family: Araceae

Habitat: A large genus of around 625 accepted species native to the tropical Americas and West Indies, growing in humid tropical forests, swamps, river banks and rocky outcrops from sea level to over 2000 m elevation. Philodendrons show diverse growth habits, from terrestrial and climbing plants to epiphytes and hemiepiphytes that begin life in the canopy and send aerial roots down to the soil.

Economic Importance: Extremely popular as houseplants and indoor foliage plants worldwide for their large, often glossy, variably shaped leaves and tolerance of low indoor light levels; numerous cultivars have been bred for leaf colour and form.

Medicinal Importance: Indigenous peoples of South America have used the resin and plant parts in traditional practices, including waterproofing blowguns and, in some tribes, ceremonial uses; the plant contains calcium oxalate crystals and should be handled with care as contact can cause mild irritation.
