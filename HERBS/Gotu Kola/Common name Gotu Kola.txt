Common name: Gotu Kola, Indian Pennywort, Asiatic Pennywort
Scientific name: Centella asiatica (L.) Urban
Family: Apiaceae

Habitat: Native to tropical regions of Africa, Asia, Australia and islands of the western Pacific; a herbaceous perennial with slender creeping stolons, growing in temperate and tropical swampy areas, though it can also be cultivated in drier, regularly watered garden soil. Considered invasive in a number of Pacific islands.

Uses: Widely consumed as a leafy green vegetable across South and Southeast Asian cuisines - for example as thankuni pata in Bangladesh and West Bengal, gotu kola malluma in Sri Lanka, and pegaga in Malaysia - and used to prepare cooling drinks in Thailand, Cambodia and Vietnam.

Medicinal Importance: Long used in traditional medicine for dermatological conditions and minor wounds, although clinical efficacy and safety have not been firmly established; adverse effects including contact dermatitis and, with prolonged use, liver disease have been reported in case studies and reviews.
