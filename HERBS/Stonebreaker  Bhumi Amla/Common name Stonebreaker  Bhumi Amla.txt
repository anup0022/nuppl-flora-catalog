Common name: Stonebreaker, Bhumi Amla, Chanca Piedra
Scientific name: Phyllanthus niruri L.
Family: Phyllanthaceae

Habitat: Phyllanthus is the largest genus in the family Phyllanthaceae, distributed throughout tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, with growth forms ranging from annual and perennial herbs to shrubs, climbers and succulents. Phyllanthus niruri is a small annual herb typically found on disturbed, moist ground in the tropics.

Medicinal Importance: Commonly called "chanca piedra" (stone-breaker) in traditional Latin American and South Asian medicine, reflecting its folk use for kidney and gallbladder stones; leaves, roots, stems and bark of Phyllanthus species contain lignans and other phytochemicals that remain of ongoing scientific research interest.

Biodiversity Value:
- Tiny stem-borne flowers are insect-pollinated; favours moist, disturbed ground and completes its life cycle quickly.

Economic & Ecological Importance:
- Widely used in traditional medicine across the tropics for kidney-stone and liver remedies, sustaining wild-collection demand.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference:
- Classical Ayurvedic name is Bhumyamalaki; documented in traditional Nighantu texts as a Yakritoddipaka (liver-supportive) dravya.
