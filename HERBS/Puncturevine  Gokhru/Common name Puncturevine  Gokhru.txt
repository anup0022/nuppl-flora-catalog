Common name: Puncturevine, Gokhru, Goathead, Caltrop
Scientific name: Tribulus terrestris L.
Family: Zygophyllaceae

Habitat: A taprooted annual herb widespread from latitudes 35 degrees S to 47 degrees N, native to warm temperate and tropical regions of southern Europe, southern Asia and Africa, and now naturalized across the Americas and Australia. Stems radiate from the crown into flat prostrate patches 10 cm to over 1 m wide; thrives in dry, loose, sandy soils and disturbed roadside ground. The hard, spiny burs readily puncture bicycle tyres and bare feet, giving the plant its common names.

Economic Importance: Considered a noxious agricultural weed in many regions because its spiny burs damage livestock (mouths and wool) and are difficult to eradicate; young leaves and shoots are eaten as a vegetable in parts of East Asia.

Biodiversity Value:
- Spiny, hard seed cases attach to hooves, tyres and shoes, aiding long-distance seed dispersal.
- Deep taproot and prostrate growth let it survive in dry, sandy soils where few other plants establish.

Medicinal Importance:
- Fruit (Gokshura) is a classical Ayurvedic herb used for urinary tract health, kidney function and as a vitality tonic.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference:
- Classical Ayurvedic name is Gokshura; its fruit is one of the ten roots of the classical Dashamoola group, documented for urinary and reproductive health.
