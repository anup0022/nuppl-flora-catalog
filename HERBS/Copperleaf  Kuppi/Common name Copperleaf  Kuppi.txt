Common name: Copperleaf, Kuppi, Indian Acalypha, Indian Nettle
Scientific name: Acalypha indica L.
Family: Euphorbiaceae

Habitat: Acalypha is a large genus of the spurge family with around 450-462 species distributed mainly through the tropics and subtropics, with roughly 60 percent of species native to the Americas and 30 percent to Africa. Acalypha indica is an erect annual herb common on waste ground, roadsides and cultivated land across India and tropical Asia.

Medicinal Importance: Indian acalypha (kuppi) has a long history of use in traditional Indian medicine as an expectorant, laxative and vermifuge, and for treating skin ailments; other species in the genus, such as the chenille plant (A. hispida), are grown ornamentally for their showy tassel-like flower spikes.

Biodiversity Value:
- Inconspicuous flower spikes are pollinated by small insects; tolerates poor soil, helping stabilise disturbed ground.

Economic & Ecological Importance:
- Used in traditional medicine in parts of Asia and Africa; otherwise mainly a common garden and roadside weed.
