Common name: Senna
Scientific name: Senna sp. (genus Senna Mill.)
Family: Fabaceae

Habitat: A large genus of 260-350 species of shrubs, small trees and perennial herbs in the legume family, native throughout the tropics with a few species in temperate regions; leaves are paripinnately compound with up to 25 pairs of leaflets, and flowers are typically yellow, arranged in racemes, pollinated mainly by buzz-pollinating bees.

Economic Importance: Several species are grown as ornamental landscaping plants; Cassia gum, extracted from the seeds of Chinese senna (S. obtusifolia), is used industrially as a thickening agent, and leaves and flowers of Siamese cassia (S. siamea) are eaten as a vegetable in Southeast Asian cuisines.

Medicinal Importance: Senna leaves and pods are a long-established herbal laxative, stimulating peristaltic contraction of the colon; senna and its active sennoside compounds remain in clinical use and study for treating constipation.
