Common name: Aloe Vera / Ghritkumari / Gwarpatha
Scientific name: Aloe vera (syn. Aloe barbadensis miller)
Type: Perennial evergreen succulent herb

Habitat:
- Likely native to the Arabian Peninsula, now naturalized worldwide
- Extremely common in home gardens and pots across India, including Kanpur
- Thrives in hot, dry climates. Loves full sun to partial shade
- Needs sandy, well-drained soil. Very drought tolerant
- Sensitive to waterlogging and frost. Perfect for UP’s climate

Economic importance:
- Medicinal: Gel from leaves used for burns, cuts, skin irritation, and sunburn. Latex used as a laxative. Widely used in Ayurveda, Unani, and Siddha. Consult a healthcare professional before medicinal use
- Cosmetic: Major ingredient in skincare, haircare, soaps, lotions, and shampoos due to moisturizing and anti-inflammatory properties
- Food & beverage: Aloe juice and gel used in health drinks and supplements
- Commercial: Large-scale cultivation in Rajasthan, Gujarat, and UP for pharma and cosmetic industries
- Ornamental: Popular potted plant for homes and offices due to low maintenance
- Air purifier: Removes benzene and formaldehyde from indoor air

Biodiversity Value:
- Tubular flowers attract sunbirds and bees; water-storing leaves let it survive long droughts.

Medicinal Importance:
- Leaf gel used for burns, cuts, skin irritation and sunburn; latex used as a laxative.
- Widely used across Ayurveda, Unani and Siddha traditional medicine systems.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference:
- Classical Ayurvedic name is Kumari (also Ghritkumari); documented in Bhavaprakasha Nighantu as a cooling dravya used for skin and digestive health.
