Common name: Indigo, Neel, True Indigo
Scientific name: Indigofera tinctoria L.
Family: Fabaceae

Habitat: A shrub 1-2 m high, native to tropical West Africa, Tanzania to South Africa and the Indian subcontinent to mainland Southeast Asia; may grow as an annual, biennial or perennial depending on climate, with light green pinnate leaves and pink or violet flowers.

Economic Importance: One of the original historic sources of indigo dye, obtained by fermenting the leaves to convert the glycoside indican into the blue pigment indigotin; still marketed today as a natural dye (known as tarum in Indonesia and nila in Malaysia) alongside its much larger-scale synthetic replacement. As a legume it is also grown as a soil-improving groundcover and rotated into fields to enrich the soil.

Biodiversity Value:
- As a legume, root nodules fix nitrogen and improve soil fertility for neighbouring plants.
- Flowers attract bees and butterflies; historically grown across large plantation areas, shaping regional farmland.
