Common Name: Sarpagandha / Indian Snakeroot

Scientific Name: Rauvolfia serpentina

Family: Apocynaceae

🌱 Growth Form & Habitat
Type: Perennial shrub.

Habitat: Moist, shady areas in tropical Asia; native to India.

Features: Glossy green leaves in whorls, small white/pink flowers, long roots.

💡 Economic & Medicinal Importance (8–10 lines)
Famous in Ayurveda and Unani medicine.

Roots used for high blood pressure treatment.

Contains reserpine alkaloid, vital in modern pharmaceuticals.

Traditionally used for insomnia, anxiety, and mental disorders.

Known as a remedy for snake bites.

Cultivated as a medicinal cash crop.

Plays a role in global hypertension drugs.

Important in traditional healing practices.

Flowers and leaves sometimes used in folk remedies.

Economically valuable due to demand in herbal and modern medicine.

Biodiversity Value:
- Small white/pink flowers attract insects; red-black fruits are eaten by birds, which help disperse the seeds.
- Overharvesting of wild roots for medicine has made natural populations rare in parts of India, prompting cultivation.

Medicinal Importance:
- Roots are the classical source of reserpine, a compound historically used to treat high blood pressure and mental agitation.
- One of the most important plants in Ayurveda and Unani medicine for hypertension.

Authentic Ayurvedic Reference:
- Sarpagandha ("snake-scented") is the classical name; long documented in traditional and Unani medicine as a root used for snakebite, insomnia and mental agitation.
